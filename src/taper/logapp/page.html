Log a day

One row per day. Saving a day you already logged corrects it rather than adding a second one. Distance and time are enough; heart rate or an effort rating make the load estimate a measurement instead of a guess.

How you feel today

Ten seconds. This is the column that makes the log a dataset rather than a diary: without it the injury model has its input and none of its outcome.

Anything hurt?

Add a body part only when it has something to say. 0 is fine, 10 is cannot run. Four or more, or anything that changed your run, counts as a flare — that is the event the model is trying to see coming.

Recent training

Click any row to load it back into the Today tab and edit it. A day showing more than one session was merged from several activities on import, so it is never read as a single continuous run.

Races and personal bests

Every race you can remember. Times here feed the fitness estimate and the record list, so an approximate date is better than none. Enter times as h:mm:ss or mm:ss.

Injury episodes

A dated flare-up, onset to resolution. Leave the resolved date empty while you are still in it. These are what the injury model is fitted against, so an approximate onset beats leaving it out.

Gaps the log noticed

Stretches where running stopped or dropped sharply. These are candidates, not conclusions — a three-week hole is equally consistent with a torn calf, a work trip or a heatwave. Promote the ones that were injuries.

Personal records

Best eligible effort at each standard distance, from races and from training days whose whole distance lands on one.

Excluded by the terrain screen

Fast, real, and not comparable to a flat road time. Shown so a missing best is never a mystery.

Export your record history

A plain text file: records, the progression at each distance, every race, and the efforts the terrain screen rejected with the reason. Readable in twenty years with no copy of this program to hand.

Where your data lives

One SQLite file, taper.db, in the directory you started this from. Copy it and you have copied everything — training days, races, symptoms, episodes and check-ins. It is ignored by git on purpose.
